## What the study is about

Continuum checks, in the background, that the person using a web session is the person who
logged in. It does this from the rhythm of typing and the way the mouse moves. When it is unsure,
it asks for a passkey (a fingerprint or PIN on your own device). We want to learn how well this
works over ordinary days of use.

## What you will do

1. **Day 1 (about 15 minutes):** install the Continuum browser extension in study mode, then type a few
   short texts and move the mouse through a short task. This creates your profile.
2. **Days 2 to 10:** use your computer as usual, with the extension running, for at least 3 sessions
   of 20 minutes or more. Sometimes you will be asked for your passkey.
3. **Twice, for 5 minutes each:** use another participant's test session on a lab computer, so we
   can measure how quickly the system notices a different person. These sessions use prepared test
   accounts, never anyone's real accounts.

## What is recorded, and what is not

- **Recorded while the extension is recording:**
  - each key you press and release, with its timing;
  - the size of each mouse step;
  - the scores the system computes, and when passkey prompts appear and how they end.
- **What the key data allows.** Because the keys are recorded in order, **the text you type could be
  reconstructed from this data** (without upper and lower case). We use it only to measure typing rhythm.
  We never read it as text, and it is deleted as described below.
- **Pausing.** You can pause all recording at any time with the extension's button, for example before
  typing something private. An icon shows when recording is on.
- **Never recorded:** passwords (password fields are masked), the pages you visit, page content and
  screenshots.
